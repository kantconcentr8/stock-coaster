# 12\. THE GOLDMAN SACHS 52-WEEK BREADTH SIGNAL

Goldman's breadth measure compares:

**the S\&P 500's distance from its 52-week high**

against

**the median S\&P 500 constituent's distance from its own 52-week high.**

The current reading is around:

### **\-15%**

near the narrowest level shown in the 2008–2026 history.

Interpretation:

> **The cap-weighted S\&P 500 has remained dramatically closer to its high than the median constituent.**

This is structural concentration.

It explains why October's short-term divergences matter.

October 21 wasn't an isolated breadth event.

It was a short-term manifestation of a much larger condition:

### **The index had become increasingly dependent upon a relatively small leadership cohort.**

# 13\. TWO WAYS CONCENTRATION CAN NORMALIZE

This is critical.

Extremely narrow breadth does **not** automatically mean the index must crash.

The spread can normalize two ways.

## BAD NORMALIZATION

Leaders collapse toward the rest of the market.

**Mega-cap ↓↓↓**

**Index ↓↓**

**Median stock ↓**

This creates index destruction.

---

## GOOD NORMALIZATION

Leaders stabilize while the median stock catches up.

**Mega-cap →**

**Median stock ↑**

**Breadth ↑**

This creates rotation and healthier participation.

Friday may contain a small hint of relative resilience underneath the cap-weighted index because \$ADD did not deteriorate proportionally to the NDX/SPX damage.

That is **not yet confirmation of rotation**.

It simply makes the next breadth response extremely important.
